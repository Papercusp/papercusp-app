How a plan ships

A plan is not done because its items are done. It is done when two independent families of evidence agree — the code truth the implementer audits, and the graded acceptance bar that someone else reads. This is the mechanism that enforces the gap between them.

One authority, three sections

A requirement is a promise, a standard of proof, and a method

There is no separate requirements store. A requirement lives as one rubric criterion, and that criterion owns three sections: Intent (the requested outcome and where it came from), Acceptance — the BAR — (what must be true, and what would falsify it), and Verification (how that outcome is demonstrated, and how to replicate the demonstration).

The distinction that matters operationally is which of the three you may revise freely. Improving a method or recording missing provenance leaves the promise intact. Changing the success condition, the falsifier, the required scope, the evidence plane, the proof depth, the mandatory role, or the passing thresholds is a change to the promise, and takes the recorded amendment path instead.

The completion path

Eight steps across three parties

The order is deliberate. The audit runs first, while the implementation is still warm and code-truth gaps are cheap to find. The rubric is authored afterwards, against as-built reality — never at plan creation, and never maintained alongside the implementation, because a bar written before the work describes the plan rather than the result. The independent grading supplies the honesty leg a self-audit structurally cannot.

Read the figure by its lanes rather than its arrows: the vertical zigzag is the design. Every drop out of the implementer's lane is a point where the system stops trusting the person who did the work.

What counts as evidence

Two citation kinds verify; two declare that nothing was verified

The per-item audit reads the source tree — never a work-item, a doc, or the memory of having written the code. Each submitted item stores its own audited SHA, timestamp and item-text hash; each verifying citation stores a Git-compatible blob SHA. At ship time the gate folds the newest audit entry per item and rechecks only what can have changed: item status, item text, citation resolution, and the cited bytes.

CitationVerifies?Requires
code counts A resolving repo-relative path; an optional line in range; an optional symbol that literally exists
test counts The same resolution rules. An implemented verdict needs at least one resolving code or test citation
doc declares non-verification ref plus reason. Not a failure — an explicit, counted statement that no code artifact backs this item
none declares non-verification reason. not-code and dropped verdicts require explanatory notes

The citation resolver proves that a cited path exists at audit and ship time, that line and symbol anchors resolve, and that cited bytes did not move unnoticed. It does not prove that a real file actually implements the item.

Plan completion runbook — “what the mechanics prove, and what they do not”

That residual gap is the entire reason for the second evidence family. A machine can confirm that bytes are where you said they were; it cannot confirm that those bytes keep the promise. So the independent grader spot-checks the implementer's citations rather than trusting the coverage summary, and the author must then say accept or reject in their own name.

The same asymmetry shows up one level down. A passing unit test of a helper cannot establish a promised end-to-end capability; the BAR's required scope, evidence plane and proof layers have to be met through the promised entry path. Documenting a tested seam for a future consumer does not discharge a promise of a working user-facing capability, and generic ship force cannot discharge an unrealized requirement.

Audit verdicts record findings; they do not themselves block shipping. Item lifecycle is the blocking truth — in-scope gaps get fixed or the item gets deliberately dropped with a reason. Out-of-scope findings never make a plan uncloseable, but a finding marked filed must name the work-item that now owns it.

The six meta-criteria a rubric is vetted against

Before an acceptance rubric may satisfy the gate, its author vets it against meta-acceptance-rubric. The six criteria are a set, not a sequence:

  • Outcomes, not diff restatements. “The function was extracted” fails; “a contradicted pass-rating refuses the emit” passes.
  • Traces to the plan goal or a Decision. An untraceable criterion is scope invention.
  • Falsifiable with evidence. Name the concrete observation that would fail it. If you cannot, rewrite the criterion.
  • Failure modes covered. At least one criterion catches the plan's plausible regression, not only the happy path.
  • Non-overlapping. Two criteria that always fail together on the same observation are one criterion.
  • Structured check preferred. Where an outcome is objectively checkable, bind it — a tests check whose files are actually run at grading time, or an instrument. Prose-only criteria for checkable outcomes are rated down on purpose; that pressure is the criterion's whole point.

For any criterion phrased as a negative, author the falsifier, not the confirming pattern: enumerate what could exhibit a violation and say what makes that enumeration complete. Grepping for the compliant helper cannot reveal a bypass that omits it, and an empty or partial enumeration is unknown, never clean.

Independence

The guard is about parties, not ordering

Vet first and the grading refuses with grader_is_sole_vetting_critic: vetting the rubric made you its only recorded critic, so rubric fitness and work compliance would both be attested by the same party. Grade first to escape that ordering and the readiness projection refuses instead with bar_snapshot_vetting_missing. Refused before, refused after — which is why every agent who meets this pair reads it as a deadlock.

It is not one. The guard unions the recorded critics across both vetting channels at the current revision and collapses them to distinct parties, refusing only when every one of them is the same party as the grader. Add a single critic who is not you and the very same grader becomes eligible — in either order.

Recruit the second critic directly: find a live session outside your lineage with coord:presence, then coord:dispatch, or work_items:create naming them in assign_to — which is born-admitted and claimable in the same write. Do not launch one: grader-eligibility.ts resolves the rubric author as the implementer and refuses anyone in that author's spawn or rebind lineage, so a grader you spawn is ineligible by construction, and you discover it only after paying for the boot and the grading pass.

Revision currency

One edit voids both cards at once

Every scorecard pins the rubric plan row's version at emit time, and any rubrics:propose of the same rubric bumps that version. This is deliberately strict: a typo-only re-propose voids the attestation too, because only re-reading the rubric can judge whether a change was material. The expensive consequence is that the attestation and a peer's completed grading pin to the same number, so a single late edit un-counts both.

Refusals

Every refusal names the repair it demands

The two families are gated independently, and only the first is waivable. Step numbers refer to the path above.

RefusalStepWhat it meansThe repair
Code truth — PLAN_ITEM_COMPLETION_GATE · PLAN_CODE_AUDIT_GATE, both default on, both waivable by force
plan_items_unfinished 1 An item is still todo, blocked, or needs-human. Finish it, or drop it deliberately with a note. Departing from the plan is allowed; doing it silently is not.
acceptance_unaudited 2 No code-truth audit has ever run for this plan. Run the first plans:audit pass.
audit_coverage_stale 2 Named items are missing, reopened, text-changed, or their cited blobs moved. Re-audit only the named items. Do not copy unchanged entries into a new pass — unlisted items carry forward with their original provenance.
audit_citations_unresolved 2 A cited path, line, or symbol no longer resolves. Repair the citation, then re-audit only those items.
Acceptance rubric — gated separately, and not waivable by force
acceptance_rubric_missing 3 No post-implementation rubric exists. Author one under the plan's class: feature-ship, bugfix, migration, or investigation.
acceptance_rubric_unvetted 5 No attestation exists at the current revision. Critique, improve, then emit the meta-scorecard against meta-acceptance-rubric citing the consult.
acceptance_ungraded 6 No complete, non-synthesized grading exists. Route the grading to a non-implementer — every criterion, with concrete evidence.
self_graded_only 6 The only grading on record is the rubric author's. Obtain one from somebody else.
grader_is_sole_vetting_critic 6 Every recorded critic collapses to the grader's own party. Add one critic outside your lineage, recorded on the other channel. Not a different ordering.
acceptance_not_recorded 7 An independent grading exists; the author's verdict does not. Re-emit the complete ratings with acceptance:{ verdict, reasoning }. Do not pass supersedes — the independent card must stay visible.
acceptance_rejected 7 The latest author verdict is reject. Address the rejection, then record a newer verdict. Reject is a real terminal judgment, not a placeholder.

The vetting check has one more property worth knowing: if no meta-rubric is registered in the workspace at all, the check disables itself rather than refusing. A gate demanding a flow the workspace cannot run is a deadlock, not a guard. The meta-rubric only needs to be registered, not ratified — ratification governs content authority, and demanding it would park every plan ship behind the owner's approval wall. Two things are exempt by design: empty-criteria investigation rubrics, and the meta-rubric itself. That exemption is one level deep by construction — there is no meta-meta-rubric, because the meta-rubric's content is owner-ratified instead.

Six false readings

Where the honest reading of what you see is the wrong one

None of these is a defect. Each is a place where a correct run looks broken, or a broken one looks fine — which is the more dangerous direction.

The attestation reads as never having landed

What you see
observation->>'vettingConsult' returns NULL on a perfectly good vetting.
What it means
The argument goes in flat and is stored as an object. Read observation.vetting — { consultId, rubricRevision } — and compare that revision to the rubric's current one while you are there. Querying the argument's own name invites a pointless re-consult of a consult that is already closed and already cited.

Your ratings are refused as off-scale

What you see
A composed scorecard rejected after you wrote every rating and its evidence.
What it means
The scale is per-rubric and does not carry over. The meta-rubric grades pass · weak · fail · not-applicable; most acceptance rubrics grade healthy · degraded · broken · unknown. You will typically write both within an hour. Read the target's ratingScale first and map deliberately — healthy→pass, degraded→weak.

A green check that proves nothing

What you see
A tests check bound to a criterion, resolving and running green.
What it means
Propose-time validation checks that the path resolves; grading-time validation refuses a pass-rating over a red run. Neither can tell whether the file tests the criterion it is bound to. A green run on the wrong file reads downstream as machine-confirmed evidence for a claim nobody verified — strictly worse than prose-only, which at least declares itself as judgment. Open the file and read its test names before binding.

A rubric that “does not exist”

What you see
The deployed operator answers rubric not found, and the list endpoint silently skips the row.
What it means
Postgres held it active and unarchived; the deployed build's older schema rejected it, and the row-mapper returns null on a parse failure while every caller drops it. A schema rejection is byte-identical in effect to an absence. Three surfaces agreed the rubric was gone — they shared one upstream cause.
Observed 2026-09-07 · filed as EI-22391548157370980

The consult that answers nobody

What you see
A vetting consult opens and returns no_qualified_responder.
What it means
The eligible pool can be entirely dead — not below the relevance floor, but every candidate ended. Read the candidates' liveness rather than the verdict alone, then recruit a critic directly off coord:presence and record them via the work-item channel.
Observed 2026-09-07 · filed as EI-22642013373311117

A transcribed critique is not third-party

What you see
An attestation citing a vettingWorkItem is refused, though the critique plainly happened.
What it means
The critic's own comment has to be on that work-item. The author faithfully transcribing what the critic said does not satisfy it, and the emit succeeded on the next attempt with the payload otherwise unchanged once the critic posted in their own name.
Observed 2026-09-07 · WI-10000622, comment authored by the critic

The scoped escape

Force is loud, narrow, and permanent

Shipping with force:{ reason } waives exactly four checks and nothing else. Every waived check, its reason, the actor and the time are appended to the plan's permanent forcedPast marker and returned by every subsequent plan read. It exists to record a deliberate exception, never to disguise one.

Force can waive

  • plan_items_unfinished
  • acceptance_unaudited
  • audit_coverage_stale
  • audit_citations_unresolved

The code-truth family only — the evidence a machine can check.

Force can never waive

  • Rubric existence — a bar must be written
  • Vetting — its own gate, outside the force path entirely
  • Independent grading — the honesty leg
  • The author's acceptance verdict — someone has to say it in their own name

The judgment family — and generic force cannot discharge an unrealized requirement.
