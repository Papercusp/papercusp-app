And the strongest lever isn't colour at all — it's a shape you already use. The mode pills carry their axis name in a filled accent cap (--accent ground, --accent-ink text, 8.40:1). Giving section headings that same cap makes a section start unmissable, and it costs no new design language: the rail and the control band end up speaking the same one.

The comprehension half

Six of your eleven sections currently say "nothing"

Contrast makes the sections findable. This is what makes them readable.

Count what the two rails are actually telling you right now. Orders: no open owner directives, no standing facts in scope, nothing blocked on you, no carry-note ever written. Activity: not waiting on any event, no locks held or waiting. That is six full sections — each with a heading, a card and a body — spent reporting absence, and under C1/C2 every one of them gets the same bordered card, the same cap, the same visual weight as the sections carrying real state.

Louder borders make that worse, not better: the eye is drawn equally to four boxes that say nothing and one that says a decision is waiting on you. So C3 does the obvious thing — a section with nothing in it stops being a card and becomes one dim line. Nothing is hidden and nothing is collapsed behind a click; the information is still there, it just stops shouting. What's left standing as a card is, by construction, what's true.

The two cluster labels do the last bit of work. "What it was told" versus "what it's carrying" is the actual distinction between these sections — mission, modes, directives and facts are inputs; walls and carry are state the agent accumulated. Naming that split turns a flat list of six headings into two ideas, which is the difference between reading the rail and scanning it.

Recommendation

Ship C3; C2 is the fallback if the caps feel loud

C3 is the one that answers both halves of what you asked. The caps and the 3.22:1 edge make section boundaries unmissable, the three-tier text ladder (1.40× heading-to-value, 1.88× value-to-key) fixes the inverted hierarchy, and the quiet-empty rule stops the added contrast from amplifying noise.

The one real risk is the accent caps reading as loud with four or five cards on screen at once. If that happens live, the cap desaturates to #2a6f93 with --fg text (5.05:1) and everything else stays — C2 and C3 are the same build with one token changed, not two designs.

Ratios are sRGB relative luminance per WCAG 2.1, computed against the popup ground --bg-popover #0d1829 and the card fill #1d3048. Specimen type sizes, spacing and content match the live rail. Nothing here is implemented yet — say the word and C3 goes in as a scoped change to AgentOrders.css and its Activity-rail counterpart, with the quiet-empty rule as a render branch in the components rather than a style.
