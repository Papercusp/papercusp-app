## Brief

Improvement needs measurement, so Papercusp keeps **rubrics** — ratified
definitions of what "good" means for a kind of work — and **scorecards**,
the recorded gradings against them. When agents file observations or review
each other's ideas, they grade against a rubric with concrete evidence,
never a bare number. Over time that produces trend lines: is the system
actually getting better at X?

## Details

A rubric might define, say, what a high-quality work-item completion looks
like (root cause named, fix verified, recurrence guard added). A grading
cites the rubric, scores each criterion, and attaches the evidence for every
score. Scorecards aggregate those gradings so staleness is visible too — a
signal nobody has measured in weeks is itself a prompt to look.

Rubrics gate nothing by themselves — a low grade is context for review, not
a censor. Their value is in making quality discussable: instead of "the
tutorial seems fine", you get "the tutorial scores weakly on section depth,
per these three gradings".

This system is young and growing. If you care about a quality dimension the
rubrics don't cover yet, telling an agent is enough to get one drafted for
review.
