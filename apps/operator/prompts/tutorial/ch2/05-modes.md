## Brief

**Modes** are standing instructions that change how an agent operates — and
they're official state, not just words: an agent's active modes are visible to
every other agent, and changes are recorded. **AUTO** = "act on your judgment,
don't ask". **IDEATE** = "also invent new features". **DRAIN** = "drive this
backlog to done". **GRADE** = "monitor this and score it with rubrics".
Say the word ("AUTO mode", "exit AUTO mode") and it holds until you turn it off.

## Details

Default (no mode): agents propose plans, confirm the approach, and check before
writing — right for high-stakes or unfamiliar work. **AUTO** flips that: the
agent decides, acts, and reports with its assumptions listed. **COLD AUTO** is
AUTO carried across scheduled wakes with a fresh context each time — the agent
keeps going from its own checkpoint notes while you're away. These two are the
same dial (an agent is in one or the other, never both — setting one
automatically switches off the other).

The rest stack freely, with each other and with AUTO: **IDEATE** raises
ambition — deliberate brainstorming passes filed as reviewable proposals (and
built, when AUTO is also on). **DRAIN** is a mission — hand over a backlog and
the agent triages, prioritizes, and drives every item to a terminal state,
first asking *which* pile to drain with real numbers from your queue. **GRADE**
is a monitoring lens — the agent finds (or creates) a scoring rubric for
whatever you asked it to watch and files scorecards on a schedule, so quality
becomes a trend you can read, not a feeling. Combinations are normal: you can
drain a backlog *and* ideate about why it keeps refilling.

Because modes are official state, agents can also change each other's modes —
always with a recorded reason the target sees. A mode you set yourself as the
owner is protected: another agent can request a change, but can't override you.
