The main root cause

The strongest finding is lifecycle closure debt.

There are 219 unattended plans whose plan items are all terminal and whose linked work items have no nonterminal work. Yet:

  • Only 4 have a code-truth audit.
  • Only 10 have an acceptance rubric.
  • Only 4 have both.
  • 209 have neither.

The shipping writer requires completed items, a current code-truth audit with resolvable citations, and a graded acceptance rubric. That is a sensible quality gate. The operational failure is that completed plans are routinely abandoned before audit → acceptance → lifecycle transition.

In many cases the implementation was finished; the closeout workflow was not.

Why plans stopped

Legitimate reasons

  • 26 reusable test fixtures or behavior drills.
  • 204 already-superseded plans.
  • 45 genuine human decisions, credentials, devices, or external operations.
  • Physical rigs, platform access, capacity, or elapsed telemetry.
  • 18 deliberately unapproved or deferred proposals.

Not good ongoing reasons

  • 201 completed plans retaining a nonterminal lifecycle.
  • 100 plan records with no executable items.
  • 18 rows pointing to missing plans or unregistered harnesses.
  • 258 readable candidates created 41–90 days ago.
  • Stale Now blocks naming old agents or old gate conditions.

The Papercusp gate changed during the audit: it was initially red, then recovered green. Any plan that still says “blocked on the red gate” must be rechecked rather than trusted as current evidence.
